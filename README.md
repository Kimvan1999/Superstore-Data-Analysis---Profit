# Superstore-Data-Analysis---Profit
Data Analyst Portfolio Project using Python and Power BI
## Project Overview
This project analyzes the Superstore sales dataset to identify the causes of low profit margins and provide data-driven recommendations for sustainable business growth.
## Executive Summary
- Although Superstore achieved sales revenue, the company's overall profitability remained relatively low, with many transactions operating close to the break-even point. This project aims to identify the key drivers behind low profit margins and provide actionable recommendations to support sustainable business growth.
- Using Python for data cleaning and exploratory data analysis (EDA), and Power BI for interactive dashboard development, the analysis examined customer behavior, discount strategies, product performance, and regional profitability.
- The findings reveal that excessive discounting is the primary factor reducing profit without significantly increasing purchase quantity. The analysis also identifies high-value customer segments, loss-making products, and underperforming regions that require immediate attention.
- Based on these insights, the project proposes practical business strategies, including category-specific discount thresholds, customer-focused marketing, and targeted optimization of products and regions. The goal is to improve profitability while supporting long-term, data-driven decision-making.

## Key Findings
### Discount is the primary factor reducing profitability
Higher discounts are associated with lower profit, while having almost no impact on purchase quantity.
### VIP customers drive business growth
VIP customers generate the majority of revenue and contribute more than the company's total profit, meaning losses mainly come from non-VIP customers.
### Several states consistently generate losses
States such as Texas, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Illinois and North Carolina contribute significant negative profit and require strategic review.
### Revenue growth does not always mean profit growth
Some high-sales orders still generate losses because excessive discounts eliminate profit margins.
